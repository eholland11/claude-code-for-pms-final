# Dispatch 4.2 — what the wiki, database and code say

Prepared 6 Oct 2026. Sources: Rook wiki (all 36 items), Rook database (callouts, pings, responders, handlers, support_tickets), `00-rook/code/dispatch-routing/`. Database data ends 6–7 Sep 2026, so it covers about 3.5 weeks after 4.2 (12 Aug) and none of the rest of September.

## Bottom line
Two different things broke in 4.2, and Priya's "mostly seasonal" read covers neither.

1. **Ping wait cut 90s → 60s: responders are timing out.** Missed pings went from about 2-3% of pings to 12-22%. Handlers describe "phone buzzed, by the time I unlocked it, gone."
2. **Ranking change: a few responders have been starved of pings.** Four responders went from about 12 pings a week to 0-2. They are still in the system and available.

Neither looks seasonal. Both started on the release date, not gradually through August.

## The numbers (pings table)
| Week starting | Pings | Taken | Turned down | Missed | Acceptance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 29 Jun – 3 Aug (6 wks) | 170-176 | 131-134 | 34-39 | 2-6 | 75-78% |
| 10 Aug (4.2 shipped Wed 12th; includes 2 pre-release days) | 177 | 96 | 43 | **38** | **54%** |
| 17 Aug | 158 | 104 | 26 | 28 | 66% |
| 24 Aug | 162 | 108 | 30 | 24 | 67% |
| 31 Aug | 165 | 120 | 24 | 21 | 73% |

- Turned-down pings did not rise. The drop is almost entirely **missed** pings (3/week before, 21-38 after).
- Acceptance is recovering but is still below the 75-78% baseline. The 10 Aug figure is blended with two pre-release days, so the true post-release low is worse than 54%.
- Callouts also dipped, from about 138/week to 110-127. That is consistent with Priya's seasonal point, but fewer callouts does not explain a lower share of pings taken.
- Pings per callout rose from 1.23 to 1.39. Fewer callouts are being taken by the first responder asked, so more are falling through to a second or third.
- Misses are not limited to one time of day. They rose across the day after 4.2 (the post-release counts are small, so this is rough).

## Finding 1: the 60-second ping wait
- Missed pings jumped from 1-3% to about 12-22% of pings, across most responders, not a few.
- Every responder had 0-4 missed pings before 4.2. Every responder with data after it had 4-9 missed, including steady ones such as Stormwrack and Sgt. Falkirk.
- Support lead (wiki, 18 Aug) said she could explain the "gone before I could answer" complaints: "we cut the ping wait from 90 seconds to 60."
- I cannot see how long responders take to answer, so the data does not prove that people were answering at 61-90 seconds. The pattern fits it and nothing else in the data contradicts it.
- Wiki roadmap item "Ping timeout tuning" is one line: no rationale, no data, no success measure. Nobody documented why 60 was chosen.

## Finding 2: some responders have been starved of pings
| Responder | Pings/week before 4.2 | Pings/week, week of 26 Aug |
|---|---|---|
| Farlight (Uptown) | about 12 | 1 |
| The Undertow (Harborside) | about 12 | 1 |
| Vesper (Old Town) | about 14 | 2 |
| Meteor Mite (Eastgate) | about 11 | 2 |

Meanwhile some responders got more: The Gale (same area as Meteor Mite) went from about 13 to 20 pings a week, Nightwell from about 15 to 23, Captain Vantage from 12 to 16. Callouts did not move away from the starved areas, so these four responders are being asked after others for the same jobs, and the pings are concentrating on a few responders.

Why this probably happens (from the code, not yet reproduced):
- `history.py`: a **missed** ping counts the same as a decline (−0.12), while a taken ping only adds +0.08. One miss takes more than a taken ping gives back.
- Nothing decays. A TODO from 2019 in `history.py` says the score might ease back toward neutral but "leaving it as-is for now".
- A low score pushes a responder down the list, so they are asked later or never. Not being asked means they can't take work and raise the score. That is a loop with no exit.
- 4.2 cut the ping wait, which created many new misses (Finding 1). That pushed several responders down the list, and the loop kept them there.
- Proximity weight was raised from 0.45 to 0.60 and recent acceptance cut from 0.40 to 0.25 in the same release. Weighting proximity more could amplify the loop for responders far from callouts, but the code alone doesn't show that. It needs checking.
- The engineering manager asked on the 4.2 wiki page (14 Aug) whether the ranking change should apply to responders who have been turning jobs down. He wrote "the config doesn't distinguish." The staff engineer was away. **That question was never answered.**

These responders did not decline more than others before 4.2. Farlight, The Undertow, and Vesper were at 15-23% declined; Meteor Mite was the highest at 29%. The starvation is not about "bad responders."

## What the handlers are saying
Support tickets (147 filed 29 Jun – 7 Sep, 107 after 4.2). Weekly tickets were 5-8 before 4.2, then 20, 27, 32, 25. The support lead's "about 3x" matches. Rough keyword grouping of the 107 (not hand-reviewed):

| Theme | After 4.2 | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Too quiet (few or no pings) | about 30 | Mostly Farlight and The Undertow (10 each), then Corporal Ashgrove and Halfmoon (5 each) |
| Too fast (gone before answering) | about 15 | Spread across 11 responders |
| Console filters (4.2 filter persistence) | about 15 | Cosmetic and useful: resets without warning, wrong filters on shared computers, no clear-all |
| Everything else | about 47 | Unrelated: Supply, dark mode, billing, search, and so on |

The support lead said two-thirds "phone never goes off" and one-third "gone before I could answer" on 26 Aug. The ticket text agrees.

Verbatims, too fast:
- "phone buzzed, by the time i unlocked it, gone." (tickets 3048, 3077, 3078)
- "had his thumb on the screen... and it switched to someone else. He was not happy. Is that expected?" (3041, 3055, 3056, 3091)
- "Is there a set amount of time before it moves on?" (3047, 3065)
- Captain Vantage, 13 Aug: "coat and one boot on... I would ask that whoever reviews this at least confirm the timing is what it is meant to be, since it felt very fast." (3043)

Verbatims, too quiet:
- "Farlight's phone has not gone off in 3 days... Nothing looks wrong on our side. Could someone check yours?" (3060)
- "She is not complaining, but she has noticed, and so have I." (3081, 3097)
- "nothing again this week. starting to wonder if im still even in the system." (3120, 3121)
- "First one in 9 days and he missed it... Given how long he had been waiting, this landed badly." (3140)

Handler wording shows they cannot tell what is wrong ("I would rather know than guess").

**Quiet tickets are the late signal.** Quiet-week tickets only began around 17 Aug and kept growing. Tickets about "gone too fast" peaked in the first week and then stayed at 2-3 a week.

## What handlers said in interviews (September, console research)
These were console-redesign interviews, not about 4.2, but all four volunteered 4.2 symptoms.
- **Aunt Dot (Vesper, 3 Sep):** "by the time he's actually got a thumb on the screen — it's gone." Also: "there have been stretches... where the phone just sits there and sits there." She noticed the fast week and the quiet weeks but hadn't connected them. Vesper's data matches (86 pings before, 17 after). She filed no ticket.
- **Mr. Ambrose (Captain Vantage, 2 Sep):** "coat on, one boot on — when it went to somebody else... there was a period where a slower-arriving response of his still landed him the job more often than not, and lately that doesn't seem to hold." This is the closest thing to evidence that responders who used to answer slowly now lose out. Also says the filter persistence works but twice reset silently.
- **Halloran (Sgt. Bulwark, 5 Sep):** mentioned in passing that "a callout went to someone else before he'd even got his boots on... sometime in August."
- **Kip (Meteor Mite and The Gale, 4 Sep):** "Meteor Mite's been texting me all week asking if something's broken because it's so quiet... The Gale hasn't stopped, like, at all, same week, same city." Kip is the clearest view of the starvation pattern: two responders in the same area, one starved and one flooded. Kip filed no ticket either.

Take-away: the two quietest-affected handlers (Dot, Kip) never filed tickets. Tickets undercount the problem.

## What this says about the handoff note
- **"Mostly seasonal, back in September."** The September data isn't in the database (it ends 6 Sep). Within what we have, acceptance fell off a cliff on the release date, not gradually, and the drop is mostly missed pings plus starved responders. The aggregate is partly recovering, but partly because starved responders now get so few pings that they no longer drag the average down. That is not the same as them recovering. Seasonality cannot be tested properly without last year's data, which we don't have.
- **"Don't make this a revert-4.2 debate."** Reasonable, but the evidence does not point at the ranking weights as such. The most direct fixes (timeout back to 90, count misses differently from declines, decay the score) are narrower than a revert.
- **"Some items were cut from 4.2."** The one I can identify is **Availability Confidence** (Q3 roadmap: target 4.2, Committed, last reviewed 30 Jun; not in the 4.2 release notes). The roadmap shows 3 committed 4.2 items; only 2 shipped. Priya said "a couple of items", so there may be others the wiki doesn't show. Also: roadmap says "Requisition approval chains" is committed for 4.3.
- **Vocabulary.** Handlers are the people filing tickets and sitting at the console; responders are the people with phones. Handlers file on behalf of responders.
